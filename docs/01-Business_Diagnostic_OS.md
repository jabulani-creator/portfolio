## 2 · Customer Intelligence

Understand who is paying, what they believe before they buy, and what makes them hesitate. The site is for these people — not for other developers.

### Who Is Paying

**SME Owner / Operator** — Owns a business with real revenue and real customers, but no visibility into why their website or Facebook page isn't converting inquiries into bookings. Has likely paid a developer before and gotten a pretty site that changed nothing.

## 4 · Revenue Leak Identification

Isolate the defensible gaps found in Sections 1–3. These become the brief for the new site.

**Leak #1 — Wrong Category, Wrong Comparison Set**
The current site puts this business in the "website builder" category, where the only competitive axis is price and aesthetics. *Fix:* Replace "Services / Portfolio" with "Digital Experience Diagnostic" (the flagship, explained and priced) and "Case Studies" (problem → discovery → leaks → decision → implementation → outcome).

**Leak #2 — The Best Proof Asset Is Hidden**
The Kwisoko diagnostic exists but sits privately — a prospect cannot encounter this thinking before a sales call. *Fix:* Publish the Kwisoko document (with permission, sensitive figures adjusted) as the site's first public case study, framed as Observation → Evidence → Decision → Implementation → Outcome — including what was actually built and what changed, not diagnosis alone.

**Leak #3 — No Priced Entry Product, So the First Ask Is the Biggest Ask**
There was no standalone, purchasable "Digital Experience Diagnostic" offer — prospects were asked to commit to a full build without a smaller, concrete first step. *Fix:* Resolved directly by the productized offer in Section 0. "Book a Digital Experience Diagnostic" becomes the single site-wide CTA, now backed by a concrete thing to book.

**Leak #4 — Visual Identity Undercuts the Positioning**
If the visual design still resembles a typical agency/freelancer portfolio, it contradicts an evidence-led, diagnostic message before a single word is read. *Fix:* Calm, editorial, documentation-style design — generous whitespace, strong typography, minimal decoration — so the look agrees with the claim.

## 5 · Strategic Blueprint & Verdict

### Overall Triage Verdict

**STRONG REPOSITIONING OPPORTUNITY — PROCEED, AT THE RIGHT ALTITUDE, WITH A SELLABLE OFFER.**

The method already works — the Kwisoko document is the proof. v2 fixed the altitude problem (Digital Experience Consultant, not Business OS). v3 fixed the concreteness problem: the flagship offer is now a named, scoped, priced thing a prospect can book, not a philosophy to be sold on a call.

### Positioning Ladder — Grow Into This, Don't Start Here

| Phase | Positioning | Trigger to Move Up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Now | Digital Experience Consultant | Current, honest positioning. Sell and deliver the productized Digital Experience Diagnostic + build. This is what this document builds toward. |
| 2 — Later | Emerging patterns across clients | After several engagements, real operational patterns repeat — noticed, not assumed. |
| 3 — Earned | Business Systems Consultant | Only once broader operational work has actually been delivered repeatedly — not before. |

### Single Highest-Leverage Quick Win

Publish the productized Digital Experience Diagnostic offer as a standalone, bookable page, and turn the Kwisoko document into the first public case study. Both are fully specified in this document — nothing further needs to be decided before building them.

## Appendix — Copy-Level Notes for the Homepage (Not Part of This Diagnosis)

These are worth using when writing actual site copy, but are language and framing decisions — not structural findings — so they don't require reopening this document:

- Lead with the customer's problem before naming the product: *"Customers are already trying to do business with you. The question is: where are they getting stuck?"*
- Frame deliverables as outcomes the client will know, not audits they're buying — e.g. *"After one week you'll know why customers drop off, what to fix first, and whether you even need a new website."*
- State implementation as equally weighted, not secondary: *"Diagnosis tells us what to build. Development makes it real."*
- Consider a "why now" paragraph: delay has a cost (lost enquiries, outdated info, manual admin), not just an opportunity cost.
- "Digital Experience Diagnostic" vs "Digital Experience Audit" remains an open copy test, not a decision — try both in real headlines.
